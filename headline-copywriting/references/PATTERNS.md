# PATTERNS: headline templates for a landing page hero

A general headline-template library, built from SWIPES.md (35 live hero headlines fetched 2026-10-06, 37 classic headlines, 7 get-clients offers). Use it to pick a shape for your own headline, then fill every slot with a specific from your reader's own words and your own product. Examples below are verbatim from the swipe set, and no headlines for any particular product are written here.

## Observations

- Median live SaaS hero headline is 6 words (mean 6.3, range 2 to 12). Fourteen of 35 sit at 5 or 6 words, and 24 of 35 sit at 4 to 8. The classic direct-response list runs longer, often 8 to 14 words, because it sold by mail and needed the headline to carry the whole offer.
- Subheads do the explaining. Live subheads run about 12 to 30 words, name the mechanism or the audience, and often list the concrete jobs ("from scheduling, payments, and meeting prep to notetaking"). The headline states the outcome or category in a breath, and the subhead says how and for whom.
- The most common live structures are: "The [category] for [who]" (Linear, Bonsai, Flair, Kit, Figma), "one place" or "only" (HoneyBook, Lemlist, Slack, Airtable), and an imperative verb plus outcome (Dubsado, Clay, Pebblely, Apollo). Among the classics the most-cited families are "How I [outcome]", "Who else wants", "Do you [painful thing]?", "To [audience] who want X but can't Y", and number plus time plus guarantee.
- Great ones leave out: the brand name (except Framer and Krea, the two weakest on specificity), adjectives like "powerful" and "seamless", the mechanism (saved for the subhead), and any hedge. Short winners (Gumroad, Photoroom, Loom) use one concrete image or number instead.
- The generic "AI" trend headlines (Notion, Vercel, Zapier, Slack, Airtable) now all say "agents". They are interchangeable. The ones that stay memorable are specific and human: "Go from 0 to $1", "Sell at first sight", "One video is worth a thousand words".
- Freelancer-tool heroes (Dubsado, HoneyBook, Bonsai) sell the jobs in order, "book clients, send contracts and get paid". Course-style get-clients offers sell a negation: "without cold emails", "without feeling salesy". Nobody in this set leads with a sample, a proof, or a number of replies, which is open ground. (Example of how to read a swipe set: when a whole category leads with the same claim and nobody leads with proof, the proof angle is open ground for you. Run the same check on whatever set you collect for your own market.)

## The 15 templates, strongest first

1. **From [pain state] to [first win]**
   - "Go from 0 to $1" (Gumroad, 5 words, subhead: "Start selling what you know, see what sticks, and get paid.")
   - "Framer is the design agent for every step from idea to launch"
   - Typical 4 to 6 words. Works when the reader has a clear zero state (no replies, no clients) and a believable first milestone.

2. **[Outcome] minus [pain]**
   - "Email & SMS marketing minus the learning curve" (Mailchimp)
   - "Get to work, with a lot less work" (Dropbox)
   - Typical 6 to 9 words. Works when the pain is well known and named by the reader themselves.

3. **The [category] for [who]**
   - "The unified platform for service businesses" (Bonsai)
   - "The product development system for teams and agents" (Linear)
   - Typical 5 to 8 words. Works for a category the reader has not heard before, so it must carry the identity of the audience. Pair with a concrete subhead.

4. **[Verb job], [verb job] and [verb result]** (the job list)
   - "Book clients, send contracts and get paid" (Dubsado)
   - "Build demand, win deals, and delight customers" (HubSpot)
   - Typical 6 to 8 words. Works when the product covers a short sequence the reader already understands. The first verb should be the reader's urgent one.

5. **[Verb] [what they want]** (the plain imperative)
   - "Find Clients" (Instantly)
   - "Create AI product photos that help you sell more." (Pebblely)
   - "Grow revenue with one connected GTM system" (Apollo)
   - Typical 2 to 9 words. Works when the reader searched for exactly this. The shortest version is a command that is also the reader's request.

6. **[Result] handled / done for you**
   - "All the work around meetings, handled." (Calendly)
   - Typical 5 to 7 words. Works when the reader wants the chore gone, and the product does the chore before they do.

7. **One place / the only [thing] you need**
   - "Manage every client, project, and payment all in one place." (HoneyBook)
   - "The only platform you need to make Outbound work." (Lemlist)
   - Typical 8 to 11 words. Works for a reader juggling several tools, such as someone who today stitches together separate tools for finding work, doing it, and getting it approved.

8. **The twisted proverb or image**
   - "One video is worth a thousand words" (Loom)
   - "Sell at first sight" (Photoroom)
   - "A website makes it real" (Squarespace)
   - Typical 3 to 7 words. Works when one familiar phrase maps to the product's core action. Risky if the phrase is cute without being clear, so the subhead must say what it is.

9. **How I [result] in [short time] / How a [new thing] made [low state] [high state]** (Caples family)
   - "How I Improved My Memory in One Evening"
   - "How a \"Fool Stunt\" Made Me a Star Salesman"
   - "Former Barber Earns $8,000 in 4 Months as a Real Estate Specialist"
   - Typical 7 to 12 words. Works when a real peer story exists and can be named with a number. Best suited to a case-study headline or a testimonial strip.

10. **Do you [painful moment]? / Are [rivals] [beating you]?**
    - "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?" (Max Sackheim, ran for decades as a control)
    - "Are They Being Promoted Right Over Your Head?"
    - "Are You Ever Tongue-Tied at a Party?"
    - Typical 6 to 9 words. Works when the reader recognises the exact moment. Name the moment in the reader's own words. (Example: for a tool that helps freelancers sell AI product photos, the moments were the empty inbox after the 75th message and the product label that came out wrong.)

11. **To [audience] who want [goal], but [blocker]**
    - "To People Who Want to Write, But Can't Get Started"
    - "For the Woman Who Is Older Than She Looks"
    - Typical 8 to 10 words. Works as a call-out that selects the reader in the first line. Naming the blocker, not just the goal, is what made it land.

12. **Specific number + short time (+ guarantee)**
    - "Read 300 Business Magazines in 30 Minutes" (Eugene Schwartz)
    - "Hands That Look Lovelier in 24 Hours, or Your Money Back"
    - "Doctors Prove 2 Out of 3 Women Can Have More Beautiful Skin in 14 Days"
    - Typical 7 to 12 words. Works when the number is true and checkable. Pairs naturally with a risk reversal.

13. **Get [clients] without [dreaded method]**
    - "Get Freelance Clients Without Feeling Salesy"
    - "Freelance Fast-Track: How to Land High-Paying Clients Without Cold Emails in 7 Days"
    - "Ditch cold pitching for good. Discover the human-centred way to land dream clients."
    - Typical 6 to 14 words. Works because the cold-message pain is the reader's own. It is also the crowded claim in the market, so your specific mechanism has to differentiate. (Example: a freelancer tool whose mechanism is sending a free sample first.)

14. **The little hidden cost / leak**
    - "A Little Mistake That Cost a Farmer $3,000 a Year"
    - "Little Leaks That Keep Men Poor"
    - "Discover the Fortune That Lies Hidden in Your Salary"
    - Typical 6 to 10 words. Works to reframe an invisible loss, such as hours of unanswered outreach or one small error that costs the customer a sale. Needs a concrete number to avoid sounding vague.

15. **Who else wants [desire]?** (bonus, narrower use)
    - "Who Else Wants a Screen Star Figure?"
    - "Who Else Wants Lighter Cake, in Half the Mixing Time?"
    - Typical 5 to 10 words. Works as a social-proof curiosity opener on a secondary section or ad rather than a modern homepage hero.

## Which templates to test first

Most cited and most used: 3 (category for who), 5 (plain imperative), 4 (job list), 7 (one place), 2 (minus pain), 10 (do you), 9 (How I). Choose by your reader's main pain. If it is a method they dread, test 13 and 10. If it is a loss they cannot see, test 2 and 14. If it is a stuck starting point, test 1. If your claim is a measurable result, test 12. Keep the headline near the 5 to 8 word band, put the mechanism in a 15 to 25 word subhead, and avoid vague category words ("AI", "powerful") and brand-first phrasing in the headline.

## Caveats

- Live data was fetched with curl and WebFetch. Upwork, Fiverr, Canva, Lovable, Midjourney and Cloudflare-protected pages blocked or served challenge pages, so they are absent.
- Landing galleries (lapa.ninja, landingfolio) and the swiped.co or swipefile.com body text were not scraped. Swiped.co appears only as index titles plus the Sackheim and Schwartz attributions.
- "Most-cited" is judged from the Schwab list frequency and the live-site frequency in this set, not from conversion data. No headline here has test results attached.
